## Search Intent

The reader has an immediate need — a launch deadline, an investor demo, a trade show, a client commitment — and they need a software developer or team to start NOW. They're not shopping for the cheapest option; they're looking for someone reliable who can start quickly and deliver.

This is pure urgency intent, bottom of funnel. Reader will convert if you can demonstrate: (1) you can start fast, (2) you're reliable, (3) the process is simple to begin.

## Proposed Structure

### H1: Need a Software Developer Fast? How to Find the Right Team Without Cutting Corners

**Opening (first 100 words — GEO answer):**
You need software built, you needed it last week, and you can't afford to spend three months vetting agencies. The good news: finding a reliable development team that can start within a week is possible. The bad news: the developers who market themselves as "fast" are often the ones most likely to blow your deadline. Here's how to find speed without sacrificing quality — and what to ask before you sign anything.

### H2: Why "Fast" and "Cheap" Are Two Different Problems
- Fast requires capacity (team available now)
- Cheap requires compromise (scope, quality, or experience)
- The three things to sacrifice: scope, budget, or timeline — you can only protect two

### H2: The 4 Fastest Ways to Get Software Built
1. **Agency with available capacity** — best for projects over $20K
2. **Freelancer via Toptal or Arc.dev** — best for single-developer tasks under $15K
3. **No-code tools (Bubble, Webflow)** — best for UI prototypes and validation
4. **AI-assisted development** — best for internal tools and simple automations

### H2: The 5-Minute Checklist Before You Commit to Anyone
- [ ] Can they start this week (not "soon")?
- [ ] Do they have a live product in their portfolio?
- [ ] Can you speak to a previous client by end of day tomorrow?
- [ ] Is the contract milestone-based?
- [ ] Is the source code yours from day one?

### H2: Red Flags That Signal Slow Delivery (Despite Fast Promises)
- "We can start immediately" with no questions about scope
- Portfolio is all Dribbble mock-ups
- No project manager mentioned, just developers
- First call is a sales call, not a scoping call
- No mention of what happens if a milestone is missed

### H2: How to Get a Realistic Scope in 48 Hours
- The 1-page brief: what your app does, who uses it, what success looks like
- What to send to get a real estimate (not a ballpark)
- The difference between a quote and a proposal

### H2: FAQ
- "Can you really start a software project in a week?" (Yes, with the right scope)
- "How much does a fast-track build cost vs standard?" (typically 15-25% premium for priority capacity)
- "What if you can't find anyone available?" (your other options)
