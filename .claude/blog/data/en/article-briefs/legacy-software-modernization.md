## Required Content
- **Direct answer in first 100 words:** Legacy software modernization is the process of updating or replacing aging business systems — typically 10+ years old — that have become too expensive to maintain, too risky to keep, or too limiting to grow with. The right strategy depends on your situation: some systems need a rebuild from scratch ($50k–$200k), others just need a replatform to cloud ($15k–$60k). The biggest mistake business owners make is waiting until a crisis forces the change — at which point options shrink and costs triple.
- **Real data:** cost of maintaining legacy systems (5–10x higher than modern equivalents per IBM study); average age of legacy systems in US SMBs (12–15 years); percentage of IT budget spent on maintaining legacy (60–75% per Gartner); modernization project success rate by strategy
- **Concrete examples:** manufacturing SMB with 15-year-old custom inventory system in Access database — migrated to Next.js web app in 16 weeks at $65,000; accounting firm with Windows desktop software (single developer, no source code) — rebuilt from scratch $110,000, parallel-run 2 months
- **Comparisons:** 4 modernization strategies (replatform, refactor, rebuild, replace) — cost, risk, timeline, when to use each
- **FAQ:** min 5 questions on cost, data migration, business continuity, timeline, how to find original code
