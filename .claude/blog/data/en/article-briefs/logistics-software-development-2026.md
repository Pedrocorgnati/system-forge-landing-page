## FAQ (min 5 questions in natural English)

1. **When does it make sense to build custom dispatch software instead of using Onfleet or Circuit?**
   When your dispatch logic is more complex than what off-the-shelf tools support: multiple service types with different routing rules, integration with a proprietary pricing system, custom driver payment calculations, or complex multi-stop optimization with time windows. Onfleet and Circuit work well for standard last-mile delivery operations; custom development is warranted when your business has specific operational logic that no generic tool handles well.

2. **What is a WMS and does my warehouse need custom software?**
   A Warehouse Management System (WMS) tracks inventory location, movement, picking, packing, and shipping within a warehouse. Off-the-shelf options (Fishbowl, Cin7, Sortly) work well for simple warehouses with standard workflows. Custom WMS development makes sense when: your warehouse has unique picking logic (zone picking, wave picking), requires integration with conveyor systems or automated equipment, or needs to connect to an ERP that off-the-shelf WMS products don't support.

3. **Does custom logistics software need to comply with ELD regulations?**
   If your custom software includes driver hours-of-service logging, it must either integrate with an FMCSA-certified ELD device or use a certified ELD module. You cannot build a non-certified ELD replacement — the FMCSA certifies specific hardware/software combinations. The safe approach is to integrate with certified ELD hardware (Samsara, Geotab) via API rather than trying to build the ELD functionality from scratch.

4. **How does route optimization work in custom dispatch software?**
   Route optimization uses algorithms to find the most efficient sequence for multi-stop deliveries — minimizing mileage, respecting time windows, and balancing driver load. Most custom solutions use Google Maps Platform (Routes API, Directions API) for basic optimization, or integrate with specialized routing engines like Routific or OptimoRoute for more complex optimization. True proprietary route optimization algorithms are expensive to build and usually only justified for very large or specialized fleets.

5. **How long does custom logistics software take to build?**
   A dispatch and driver app: 12–20 weeks. A WMS with barcode scanning: 16–24 weeks. A full supply chain visibility platform: 6–12 months. Logistics software projects often take longer than expected because of integration complexity (ELD, EDI, ERP) and the need for extensive testing with real-world operational scenarios.

6. **What is EDI and does my custom software need to support it?**
   EDI (Electronic Data Interchange) is a standardized format for exchanging business documents (purchase orders, invoices, shipping notices) between companies. If you're a 3PL or carrier doing business with major retailers (Walmart, Target, Amazon), you almost certainly need EDI support. EDI integration adds $10,000–$25,000 to a project depending on the transaction sets required, but it's often a business requirement rather than an optional feature.
