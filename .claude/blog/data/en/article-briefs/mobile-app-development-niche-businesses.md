# Brief: Mobile App for Small & Niche Businesses — When You Need One, Real Costs, App vs PWA (2026)

## Editorial Goal

Cut through the "every business needs an app" agency pitch with a decision-tree article that filters out the 70% of small businesses that should NOT build a native app and serves the 30% that should. Differentiator: explicit **app vs PWA vs web + SMS** decision matrix grounded in TCO (build + iOS/Android annual fees + OS upgrade cadence + store rejections), with USD pricing for each path and the honest test: "if push notifications aren't core to the value, you don't need a native app."

## Target Persona

- **Primary:** Owner of a US small business (service, retail, fitness, food, professional services) being pitched a $25–100K mobile app by an agency and trying to decide if it's worth it.
- **Secondary:** Founder of a niche/vertical SaaS or marketplace where the customer experience truly depends on push, offline, camera, or background processing.
- **Typical trigger:** "An agency quoted us $60K for an app — is that real?" / "Our competitor launched an app, do we need one?" / "We want loyalty + push + scheduling — app or website?"

## Direct Answer (GEO — first 100 words)

A native iOS + Android app for a small US business costs **$25,000–$120,000** to build, plus **$8,000–$30,000/year** to maintain (OS upgrades, store policy changes, bug fixes, server). Most small businesses don't need a native app — a fast mobile website or PWA (Progressive Web App) delivers 80% of the value at 20% of the cost ($6–18K build, $1–3K/year). Native is justified only when **push notifications, offline use, camera/GPS, or background processing** are central to the value, or you serve repeat users 5+ times per month. Plan **12–22 weeks** for build, plus **2–4 weeks every year** for OS-mandated maintenance.

## FAQ (minimum 5)

1. **How much does a small business mobile app actually cost in the US in 2026?** Build runs $25,000–$120,000 depending on complexity. Add $8,000–$30,000/year ongoing (OS updates, store policy changes, bug fixes, server). A bare MVP on one platform with React Native lands $25–45K. Both platforms with custom design and integrations: $50–80K. Offline-first or real-time multi-role: $90–120K+.

2. **Should I build a native app or a PWA?** PWA first, native later if metrics justify. PWAs install from the browser (no store fees), support push on iOS 16.4+ and Android, work offline with service workers, and cost 60–80% less to build and maintain. Native wins only when you need deep iOS/Android features (background processing, ARKit, complex Bluetooth, App Store visibility) or your users open the app 5+ times/month.

3. **Why is mobile app maintenance so expensive?** iOS releases a major version every September that breaks something. Android pushes target SDK requirements that force annual rebuilds. React Native, Expo, Flutter, and native dependencies have quarterly upgrade cadences. Apple and Google change store policies (privacy labels, data safety form, ATT) regularly. Skip a year and your app gets pulled from the store — there's no "set and forget" path.

4. **React Native vs Flutter vs native — what's the difference for cost?** React Native + Expo SDK 53: single codebase, fastest time-to-market, OTA updates, ~30–40% cheaper than native. Flutter: single codebase, excellent UI consistency, similar cost. Native Swift + Kotlin: best performance and platform feel, 1.7–2x the build cost, 2x maintenance because you're shipping two codebases. For most small business apps, React Native + Expo is the right call.

5. **Do I need to be in the App Store?** Probably not. Discovery via App Store / Play Store for net-new businesses is negligible — most installs come from your existing channels (web, email, in-store QR). If your customers already know you, a PWA installable from your website performs just as well for retention. The store matters if you target consumer discovery (entertainment, dating, utilities).

6. **What about hybrid frameworks like Capacitor or Cordova?** OK for content-heavy apps that are mostly a web view with a few native features. Not appropriate for performance-sensitive UIs or apps with complex animations. If you go hybrid, prefer Capacitor (Ionic) over Cordova — modern, better tooling.

7. **How long from kick-off to App Store launch?** MVP on one platform: 10–14 weeks (build) + 1–4 weeks (store review, with chance of rejection). Both platforms with custom design: 16–22 weeks total. Complex offline-first: 24–32 weeks. Plan 2–6 weeks of review buffer — first-time submissions get rejected ~40% of the time on Apple for metadata/permissions issues.
