## FAQ (min 5 questions in natural English)

1. **What does custom software development cost in Raleigh-Durham?**
   Local Triangle agencies typically run $90–$175/hour — mid-range by US standards. A focused web application costs $25,000–$80,000; HIPAA-compliant applications add a 20–30% premium for compliance architecture. Nearshore partners in the same EST timezone cost $40–$85/hour for equivalent quality. Your specific number depends on scope, compliance requirements, and integration complexity.

2. **Does my Research Triangle company need HIPAA-compliant software?**
   If you're handling protected health information (PHI) — patient records, health data, clinical trial data — yes. HIPAA applies to covered entities (healthcare providers, health plans) and their business associates (software vendors handling PHI on their behalf). Life sciences companies handling patient data in clinical trials also face HIPAA implications even if they're not healthcare providers. Any software touching PHI needs proper BAAs, encryption, access controls, and audit logging.

3. **What makes the Research Triangle different from other tech markets?**
   The concentration of life sciences, pharma, and enterprise software companies creates demand for highly technical software — HIPAA-compliant systems, scientific data platforms, enterprise integrations, and SaaS products for professional markets. It's less consumer-facing than Silicon Valley and more focused on B2B and research-driven software. Vendors working in RTP need genuine technical depth, not just web design skills.

4. **Are there software development companies that specialize in life sciences in Raleigh?**
   Yes, there are specialized life sciences software firms in RTP. For general-purpose software (web apps, automation, integrations), the broader development market serves Triangle companies well — including nearshore partners with HIPAA experience. The key is finding a vendor who understands HIPAA compliance and can work within your data security requirements, not necessarily one who only builds life sciences software.

5. **How can I evaluate a software vendor's technical depth before hiring them?**
   Ask for a technical architecture discussion with their lead developer (not just a proposal from a salesperson). Review their GitHub or portfolio for code quality. Ask for references from clients in similar technical domains. Have them explain how they would approach your specific integration or compliance challenge — a technically capable team will give you a substantive answer, not a generic one.
