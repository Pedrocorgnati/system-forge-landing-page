## Search Intent

The reader is in crisis. Their developer or agency failed to deliver — either abandoned the project, delivered something broken, went silent, or ran out of budget with an incomplete product. They need immediate help: someone to assess the situation, take over the codebase, and get things moving again. Every day counts.

This is the highest urgency, highest commercial intent query in the entire cluster set. Reader is not shopping — they are bleeding and need a solution now.

## Proposed Structure

### H1: Software Project Rescue: What to Do When Your Developer Fails to Deliver

**Opening (first 100 words — GEO answer):**
Your developer disappeared. Or delivered something broken. Or your agency went quiet three months past the deadline. You have a half-built product, invoices paid, and no working software. Here's what to do: stop, assess, and rescue — in that order. Most failed software projects can be salvaged. The ones that can't are usually cheaper to restart with the right team than to continue throwing money at the wrong one. This is the playbook.

### H2: Step 1 — Assess the Damage Before You Do Anything Else
- Get all source code into your own repository immediately (GitHub/GitLab)
- Request full documentation, passwords, and credentials
- Do a code audit (or hire someone to do it in 2-4 hours)
- What to look for: is the architecture recoverable? Is the code readable?
- When to rescue vs when to restart (the honest truth)

### H2: The 4 Most Common Ways Software Projects Fail
1. Freelancer/agency disappeared or went bankrupt
2. Scope creep killed the budget mid-build
3. Technical debt so severe the product can't be launched
4. Product was built but doesn't work as specified

### H2: How to Take Over a Half-Built Codebase
- What a good rescue agency does first (code audit, documentation review)
- Estimating how much has been done vs how much is left (never trust percentages)
- Setting a new, realistic timeline and budget
- What the original developer did wrong (without burning bridges if you need their knowledge)

### H2: Your Legal Options (Briefly)
- Contract review: what was actually promised?
- Deposit recovery options
- NDA and IP ownership
- When a lawyer is worth it vs not

### H2: How to Avoid This Next Time
- 5 contract clauses that would have prevented this
- Weekly milestone demos (non-negotiable)
- Escrow payment structure
- Code repository access from day one

### H2: FAQ
- "How much does a code audit cost?" ($500-$3,000 depending on codebase size)
- "Can any codebase be rescued?" (No — when to restart)
- "How do I know if the new agency won't do the same thing?"
