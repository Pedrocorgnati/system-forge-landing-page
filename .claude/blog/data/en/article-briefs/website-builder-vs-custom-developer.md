# Brief: Website Builder vs Custom Developer: Which One Does Your Business Actually Need?

## Editorial objective

Capture US SMB buyers in the "should I use Wix/Squarespace/Webflow or hire a developer?" decision stage. Give a direct, honest answer with a practical decision framework — not a fence-sitting "it depends." Interlink to vertical website guides and drive diagnostic or WhatsApp CTAs for buyers who determine they need a developer.

## Target persona

- **Primary:** Small business owner who has heard about website builders and is deciding between DIY (Wix/Squarespace) and hiring a professional developer. Budget-conscious, not technical.
- **Secondary:** Business owner who has had a website builder for 2–3 years and is hitting its limits — slow site, poor SEO, integration limitations, or a look that no longer feels professional.
- **Decision trigger:** Starting a business, rebranding, or hitting a specific wall with their current website (usually SEO, integrations, or design quality).

## Direct answer (GEO — first 100 words)

For most small businesses, **website builders work fine at the start** — but most outgrow them within 2–3 years. Wix and Squarespace cost $200–$500/year and get you online fast. Custom development costs $5,000–$25,000 upfront but gives you better SEO performance, unique design, and the ability to add integrations (booking, CRM, payments) that builders can't support. Webflow sits in the middle: professional templates with more flexibility, but still limited for complex functionality. The right answer depends on your business type, growth stage, and how important your website is to generating revenue. This guide gives you a clear decision framework.

## FAQ (min 5 questions in natural English)

1. **Is Wix or Squarespace good enough for a professional business?**
   For businesses that mainly need an online presence — a portfolio, a menu, a service description, and a contact form — yes, they're good enough. The problems emerge when you need to rank competitively in Google (builder sites are slower and less flexible for technical SEO), add custom integrations, or present a design that differentiates your brand from competitors who are using the same templates.

2. **How much better is a custom website for SEO?**
   Significantly better, if built well. Website builders generate excess HTML, have slower load times, and offer limited control over technical SEO elements like schema markup, URL structure, and Core Web Vitals scores. A well-built custom website (Next.js, Astro, or similar) typically scores 80–95 on Google PageSpeed vs. 50–70 for Wix/Squarespace. In competitive markets, that difference directly affects your Google ranking and organic traffic.

3. **What is Webflow and is it better than Wix?**
   Webflow is a no-code website builder designed for professional designers and marketing teams — it produces cleaner code and faster sites than Wix or Squarespace. It's a good middle ground for businesses that want template-based websites with more design control and better SEO performance. It doesn't support complex backend logic (user authentication, custom databases, payment workflows) — for those, you still need custom development.

4. **At what point should I switch from my website builder to a custom site?**
   Consider switching when: (1) your Google ranking stagnates despite good content, (2) you need a specific integration the builder doesn't support, (3) your website looks identical to competitors using the same template, or (4) your page speed scores are consistently below 60. Most businesses hit at least one of these walls within 3 years of launching on a builder.

5. **How much does it cost to migrate from a website builder to a custom site?**
   A migration from Wix/Squarespace to a custom platform costs $5,000–$15,000 depending on how much content and functionality you have. Done properly, it includes SEO migration (preserving URL structure and redirects), performance optimization, and content transfer. Poorly managed migrations can cause significant ranking drops — so work with a developer who has done it before.

6. **Can I build an e-commerce store on Wix or Squarespace?**
   For simple product catalogs with standard checkout, yes — both platforms support basic e-commerce. For inventory management, multi-variant products, custom shipping rules, B2B pricing, or high-volume stores, you'll hit their limitations quickly. Shopify is a better off-the-shelf option for e-commerce; custom development (WooCommerce or a fully custom solution) makes sense for complex catalogs or unique business models.
