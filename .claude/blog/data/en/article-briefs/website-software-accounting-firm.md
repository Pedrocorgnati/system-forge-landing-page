# Brief: Website + Software for Accounting Firms — Client Portals, Billing Automation, Real Costs (2026)

## Editorial Goal

Cover a commercial-buyer angle missing from US accounting trade content: the firm website **as a revenue-protection system** — secure portal, e-sign engagement letters, doc request lists, automated billing, status tracking — not a generic "best accounting websites" listicle. Differentiator: position the build around realization rates, capacity per CPA, and seasonal surge (Jan–Apr), with transparent USD pricing. Persona: managing partner or operations manager at a US accounting firm (5–60 staff) deciding between off-the-shelf practice management vs. custom website + portal layered on QuickBooks/Xero/Drake/UltraTax.

## Target Persona

- **Primary:** Managing partner or operations manager at a US accounting/tax/bookkeeping firm with 5–60 staff, fee revenue $750K–$15M, currently using a mix of QuickBooks Online + Karbon/Canopy/TaxDome + email + Dropbox.
- **Secondary:** Founder of a virtual CFO firm or accounting SaaS startup needing a client-facing portal that integrates with their accounting stack.
- **Typical trigger:** "We lost the client onboarding files again" / "Tax season killed us — we need a real doc collection system" / "Clients want to pay by card, not check, but we can't justify the QBO fees."

## Direct Answer (GEO — first 100 words)

A website + client portal for a US accounting firm costs **$12,000–$65,000** to build, depending on portal complexity (doc collection, e-sign, secure messaging), billing/payments integration (ACH, card, Stripe/Authorize.net), and practice tool integrations (QuickBooks, Xero, Drake, UltraTax, Karbon). Off-the-shelf practice management (TaxDome, Karbon, Canopy) costs **$50–$120/user/month** and covers 70% of firms. Custom build is justified when you have firm-specific workflows (multi-entity tax, fractional CFO bundles, white-label for clients) or want to consolidate 4+ disconnected tools. Timeline: 8–16 weeks. Top mistake: building a "pretty marketing site" with no portal and treating compliance (SOC 2, GLBA, MFA) as an afterthought.

## FAQs (min 5 natural questions)

1. **How much does a website + client portal cost for an accounting firm in the US?**
   Realistic 2026 ranges: marketing site alone $4–12K; marketing site + basic portal (doc upload, e-sign, secure messaging) $12–25K; full portal with billing, RBAC, QuickBooks/Xero sync, status tracking, and audit log $25–45K; white-label or multi-firm fractional CFO portal $45–120K. Annual maintenance runs 15–20% of build cost. Off-the-shelf practice management (TaxDome, Karbon, Canopy) at $50–120/user/month covers 70% of firms — custom build is justified when you need workflows those tools can't model (multi-entity tax bundles, fractional CFO white-label, deep custom integrations).

2. **When does custom software beat TaxDome or Karbon for an accounting firm?**
   Three concrete tests: (1) **firm-specific workflows** that TaxDome's templates can't model (e.g., multi-entity SMB tax bundles with shared docs, fractional CFO recurring deliverable cadence, RIA-affiliated CPA workflows); (2) **white-label or embedded portal** — you sell portal access as part of fractional CFO retainers or you're a virtual firm with branded sub-portals; (3) **tool consolidation** — you're paying for 5+ disconnected tools (portal, billing, e-sign, doc storage, project mgmt) and a $30–45K custom build saves $25K+/year in SaaS fees while removing data sync friction. If none of the three apply, stay on off-the-shelf — the math doesn't work.

3. **What about HIPAA? Does my accounting website need HIPAA compliance?**
   No — HIPAA covers Protected Health Information (PHI) handled by covered entities (providers, plans, clearinghouses) and their business associates. Accounting firms generally do not handle PHI. The right frame for accounting firms is **GLBA Safeguards Rule** (FTC, updated 2023), **IRS Pub 5708 WISP** (mandatory for PTIN holders since 2024), and increasingly **SOC 2 Type II** for firms selling to enterprise clients. Anyone telling you "we built it HIPAA-level" without explaining GLBA, WISP, and MFA enforcement is selling marketing language, not compliance.

4. **Should I use Stripe, Authorize.net, or QuickBooks Payments for client billing?**
   For most firms: **Stripe** for the lowest friction and best developer experience (2.9% + 30¢ card, 0.8% ACH capped at $5 — best in class for ACH). **Authorize.net** when you have high-ticket recurring engagements where the $25/month fee + lower per-transaction processing pays off. **QuickBooks Payments** only if you're already deep in QBO Advanced and want one-click reconciliation — but the 1% ACH (capped at $10) is higher than Stripe. Heavy recurring firms ($500K+/year processed): negotiate custom rates with all three; you'll typically get 0.5–1.0% off card and ACH caps lifted.

5. **What integrations matter most for an accounting firm portal?**
   In rough order of value: (1) **QuickBooks Online + Xero** for SMB clients and **Drake / UltraTax / ProConnect / CCH Axcess** for tax workflows; (2) **DocuSign or Adobe Sign** for engagement letters (or build into the portal natively); (3) **Stripe or Authorize.net** for ACH + card; (4) **Karbon or Asana** if you want to keep their workflow engine; (5) **Zapier or Make** as a fallback for the 30+ smaller tools (Calendly, HubSpot, Mailchimp). Avoid early integration with anything that doesn't have a stable API — IRS e-services, state tax systems, and many legacy tax tools require scraping or RPA, which is brittle and expensive to maintain.

## Reader Objections (min 3)

1. **"We already have TaxDome / Karbon / Canopy, why would we build custom?"** → Often the right answer. Off-the-shelf covers 70% of US firms. Custom is justified only when you have firm-specific workflows the templates can't model, white-label/multi-firm needs, or you're paying for 5+ tools that a $30–45K consolidated build would replace. Run the math: SaaS spend × 5 years vs. build cost + maintenance. If TaxDome at $50/user × 20 staff × 5 years = $60K covers your needs, custom doesn't pencil.
2. **"$25–45K seems steep for a portal"** → It's not a "portal" — it's a regulated, multi-integration, audit-traced system handling client PII and money movement during seasonal surge. Compare honestly: TaxDome at $50/user × 20 staff × 12 months = $12K/year, $60K over 5 years, with no IP ownership and feature roadmap controlled by vendor. Custom build $35K + $6K/year maintenance = $65K over 5 years, with IP, customization, and consolidation of 3–4 tools you currently pay for separately.
3. **"Won't this be a security/compliance nightmare?"** → It's a managed risk, not a black box. The vendors you pick (Stripe, AWS, Vercel, Clerk, DocuSign) carry their own SOC 2 attestations. Your job is to (1) write a WISP per IRS Pub 5708 (mandatory anyway since 2024), (2) enforce MFA on every login, (3) encrypt at rest + in transit (table stakes — AWS RDS + S3 do this by default), (4) maintain an audit log for every doc access and status change, (5) document your incident response plan. If your partner can deliver these five, you're meeting GLBA Safeguards expectations.
