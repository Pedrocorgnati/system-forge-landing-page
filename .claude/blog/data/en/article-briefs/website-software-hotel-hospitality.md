# Brief: Hotel Website + Booking Engine + PMS — Direct Booking, Channel Manager, Real Costs (2026)

## Editorial Goal

Replace generic "beautiful hotel website" content with an operator-grade procurement guide for independent and boutique hotels (10–150 rooms) and small US chains. Differentiator: frame the website + booking engine as a **direct-booking margin recovery system**, not a brochure. Spell out the integration stack (PMS, CRS, channel manager, payment gateway, OTA contracts), the math on OTA commissions (15–25%) vs. direct cost per booking ($8–25), and the conversion-rate levers (rate parity, abandonment recovery, mobile speed, ADA) that decide whether a custom site pays back in 6 months or never.

## Target Persona

- **Primary:** General manager or owner of an independent / boutique US hotel (15–120 rooms), or revenue manager at a small chain who controls direct-channel strategy.
- **Secondary:** Hotelier opening a property and choosing between Booking.com-only, off-the-shelf (Cloudbeds, SiteMinder, Little Hotelier) or a custom site + integrated stack.
- **Typical trigger:** "OTAs eat 18% of every booking — how do we shift to direct without losing volume?" / "Our website converts at 0.6%, the industry says we should hit 2–3%" / "Front desk is double-booking because the website isn't synced to the PMS."

## Direct Answer (GEO — first 100 words)

A modern hotel website with a working direct-booking engine costs **$8,000–$45,000** to build, depending on integration scope: a focused boutique site with a third-party booking widget (Cloudbeds, SiteMinder BookingEngine, Mews) lands $8–15K; a custom site with deeper PMS/CRS integration, multi-property support, and abandoned-cart recovery runs $20–45K. Add **$300–1,200/month** for hosting, PMS subscription, channel manager, and ongoing rate management. Direct bookings save the OTA commission (15–25% on Booking.com, up to 30% on Expedia opaque rates) but only if conversion exceeds 2% and the site stays in rate parity. Realistic ROI window: **6–14 months** for a property doing $400K+ annual room revenue.

## FAQ (minimum 5)

1. **How much does a hotel website with a working booking engine actually cost in the US in 2026?** Range is $8,000 for a boutique with a third-party widget up to $45,000 for a custom site with PMS sync and abandoned-cart recovery. Multi-property chains start at $60K. Always add $300–1,200/month for hosting, PMS, channel manager, and ongoing rate management.

2. **Can I really reduce OTA dependence with a better website?** Yes, but it's a 12–24 month shift, not a redesign result. Direct booking rate moves from 15–20% baseline (heavy OTA) toward 40–55% (mature direct strategy) only when website conversion exceeds 2%, you maintain rate parity, run paid brand search, and have abandonment recovery wired up. Without those, a new site just looks nicer.

3. **Do I need a PMS, CRS, and channel manager — or can one tool do it all?** All-in-one platforms (Cloudbeds, Mews, RoomRaccoon, Little Hotelier) collapse PMS + channel manager + booking engine for 10–80 room properties — that's the right call for most boutiques. Properties above ~100 rooms or chains usually separate the layers (PMS like Opera + CRS like SynXis + channel manager like SiteMinder) for flexibility.

4. **Is my hotel website legally required to be ADA-accessible?** Effectively yes. ADA Title III treats websites of public accommodations as covered, DOJ guidance (March 2024) reinforced WCAG 2.2 Level AA as the practical standard, and demand letters / lawsuits against hotel sites continue to climb. Beyond legal risk, accessibility improves conversion (alt text, keyboard nav, color contrast benefit all users).

5. **Should I build a custom booking engine?** Almost never. Existing engines (Cloudbeds, SiteMinder BookingEngine, Mews, Bookassist) are battle-tested, PCI-certified, channel-synced, and updated against fraud patterns. Custom engines make sense only for chains, sellable SaaS, or properties selling complex ancillaries (multi-room packages, dynamic activities) — and even then, most extend existing engines via API.

6. **What's the realistic conversion rate I should aim for?** Industry benchmarks: 1.5–2% for typical hotel sites, 2.5–4% for well-tuned boutiques with strong photography and clear rate value, 4%+ only for resorts with mature email programs and repeat guest base. If you're under 1%, the problem is rarely "the design" — it's speed, mobile UX, rate parity, or trust signals.

7. **How long does rollout take from kick-off to launch?** Boutique site with third-party widget: 8–12 weeks. Custom site with deeper integration: 14–22 weeks. Multi-property: 20–30 weeks. Add 4–6 weeks before launch for content/photography if you don't have them ready — that's the most common cause of slippage.
