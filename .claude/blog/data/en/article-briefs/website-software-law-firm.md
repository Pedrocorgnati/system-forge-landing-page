## Direct answer (GEO — first 100 words)

A professional law firm website in the US costs **$5,000–$25,000** depending on size and features. A simple attorney website with bio pages, practice areas, and contact forms runs $5,000–$10,000. Adding a client intake form with automated follow-up and a basic document portal brings the cost to $10,000–$20,000. Custom case management software or billing automation systems range from $20,000–$75,000. Most law firms are best served starting with a strong website and a structured intake flow — then layering in custom software as they grow. This guide covers what US law firms typically build, what it costs, and what to prioritize.

## FAQ (min 5 questions in natural English)

1. **How much should a law firm spend on its website?**
   A solo attorney or small firm should budget $5,000–$12,000 for a professional website that builds trust and captures leads effectively. Firms with multiple practice areas, multiple attorney bios, or complex intake needs should expect $12,000–$20,000. Anything significantly cheaper usually means a template that makes your firm look like every other law firm online — which is the opposite of what a website should do.

2. **Does my law firm website need to comply with state bar advertising rules?**
   Yes. Most state bars regulate attorney advertising — including websites. Rules vary by state but typically cover required disclaimers ("results not guaranteed," "prior results do not guarantee similar outcome"), restrictions on testimonials, and rules about describing your firm as "the best" or "top-rated." A developer who specializes in legal websites will know your state's specific requirements; a generic agency probably won't.

3. **Is Clio worth it, or should I build custom software?**
   Clio is excellent for most law firms — especially those under 20 attorneys who don't have highly specialized workflows. Custom software makes sense when: your practice has unique billing rules Clio can't handle, you want deep integration with a custom client-facing portal, or you're spending $10,000+/year on multiple SaaS tools that don't talk to each other. Most firms should start with Clio and build custom only when a specific pain point justifies it.

4. **What is a client portal and does my firm need one?**
   A client portal is a secure, private space where clients can log in to view their case documents, send messages, sign agreements, and see billing statements — without emailing sensitive information back and forth. Firms handling real estate, family law, estate planning, or any matter with frequent document exchange should have one. Clio has a built-in portal; custom portals make sense when you need tighter branding or specific integrations.

5. **Do I need to worry about ADA compliance for my law firm's website?**
   Yes. Law firm websites — like all commercial websites — are subject to ADA Title III, which courts have increasingly interpreted to cover websites. An ADA-compliant website follows WCAG 2.1 AA guidelines: proper heading structure, alt text for images, keyboard navigation, color contrast ratios, and screen reader compatibility. Non-compliant websites have triggered demand letters and lawsuits against professional services firms including law firms.

6. **What makes a law firm website convert visitors into consultations?**
   The highest-converting law firm websites have: a clear practice area focus on the homepage (not "we do everything"), specific results or case outcomes listed prominently (subject to bar rules), a friction-free consultation request form that asks for just enough information to qualify the inquiry, fast load time on mobile (50%+ of legal searches happen on mobile), and strong local SEO with Google Business Profile integration.
