# Brief: Restaurant Website + Direct Ordering — Stop the Marketplace Tax, Real Costs (2026)

## Editorial Goal

Reframe the restaurant website topic from "online presence" to **marketplace tax recovery**: DoorDash, UberEats, and Grubhub charge **15–30% commissions** on every order, plus delivery fees, plus customer ownership. A direct-ordering website + POS integration shifts margin back to the operator — but only if conversion, speed, and operational discipline (kitchen tickets, packaging, drivers) hold up. Differentiator: dollarize the breakeven, name the real integration stack (Toast, Square, Clover, Lightspeed + Stripe + DoorDash Drive), and warn about the pitfalls (POS sync delays, double orders, fraud, chargebacks). Persona: restaurant owner or small chain operations director.

## Target Persona

- **Primary:** Owner of a US independent restaurant or small chain (1–8 locations) being eaten alive by DoorDash/UberEats fees, plus operators of fast-casual, ghost kitchen, or pizza concepts where takeout/delivery is 30%+ of revenue.
- **Secondary:** Restaurant group COO / ops director standardizing direct-ordering across multiple brands or locations.
- **Typical trigger:** "We did $480K through DoorDash last year and paid $96K in fees — what would direct ordering actually save us?" / "Our POS (Toast / Square) has online ordering but the conversion is terrible" / "We want loyalty + direct ordering + reservations, can we get all three on one site?"

## Direct Answer (GEO — first 100 words)

A restaurant website with direct online ordering and POS integration costs **$6,000–$30,000** to build in the US in 2026: a clean themed site with embedded ordering (Toast Online Ordering, Square Online, ChowNow) lands $6–12K; a custom site with deeper POS integration, loyalty, and SMS marketing runs $18–30K. Monthly stack: **$200–800** (hosting, POS subscription, payment processing, SMS). Direct ordering saves the marketplace commission (15–30% per order) but only if your conversion rate exceeds 4% and operations can handle the additional channel. Realistic breakeven: **3–9 months** for a restaurant doing $300K+ in delivery/takeout annually.

## FAQ (minimum 5)

1. **How much does a restaurant website with direct online ordering cost in 2026?** $6,000–$30,000 to build. POS-embedded ordering (Toast, Square): $6–12K. Custom site with deeper POS integration + loyalty: $18–30K. Multi-location: $30–50K. Add $200–800/month for hosting, POS subscription, payment processing, SMS, and optional driver platform.

2. **Will direct ordering really save money versus DoorDash/UberEats?** Yes, but only with operational discipline. Marketplaces charge 15–30% commission per order. Direct ordering costs 3–6% (payment processing + tech + driver if self-delivered). For a restaurant doing $300K+/year through marketplaces, breakeven is typically 3–9 months. The trap: if your direct ordering conversion is under 2%, the marketing spend to drive traffic eats the savings. Successful direct-ordering restaurants run paid brand search, SMS, loyalty, and email — it's a program, not a feature.

3. **Should I use my POS's built-in ordering or a third party?** For 1–3 locations on Toast or Square, the native ordering is usually the right call — single subscription, kitchen tickets sync automatically, no double entry. For multi-brand groups, premium concepts, or operations with complex modifiers/upsells, ChowNow, Olo, or Owner.com may convert better and offer more marketing tools. Custom is justified only for restaurant groups, ghost kitchens, or sellable concepts.

4. **What about delivery — do I need my own drivers?** Three honest options. Self-delivery: best margin, requires drivers + insurance + dispatching, only viable in dense delivery zones. DoorDash Drive / Uber Direct / Relay: white-label, you keep the customer, pay ~$7–10/delivery (vs. 25%+ commission). Marketplace-only delivery: you cede customer ownership and margin entirely. Most restaurants doing direct ordering well use a hybrid (self-deliver lunch rush, white-label dinner, marketplace for discovery only).

5. **Is my restaurant website ADA-required?** Effectively yes. ADA Title III applies to public-accommodation websites, and restaurant chains (Domino's most famously) have lost lawsuits. WCAG 2.2 AA is the practical standard. Beyond legal exposure, accessibility improves mobile UX broadly — alt text, color contrast, keyboard navigation help all users.

6. **What's the realistic conversion rate for direct ordering?** 2–3% baseline for typical restaurants, 4–6% for well-tuned operations with photography, fast load, guest checkout, and clear pricing, 6%+ only for category leaders with mature loyalty programs. If you're under 2%, the issue is usually mobile speed, forced account creation, or hidden fees at checkout.

7. **How long does the project take?** Themed site + POS-embedded ordering: 4–8 weeks. Custom site + deeper POS integration + loyalty: 10–18 weeks. Multi-brand or multi-location with custom marketing: 20–30 weeks. The ongoing operational rollout (training staff, packaging, driver workflow, marketing) takes another 60–90 days before the numbers settle.
