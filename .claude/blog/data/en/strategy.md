## 2. Editorial Pillars

### Pillar 1: Core Service (custom software, MVP, SaaS, consulting)

**Goal:** Rank for high-intent commercial queries. Convert readers into discovery calls.

Topics include: custom software development process, MVP development, SaaS product building, fractional CTO services, technical due diligence, web app vs mobile app strategy.

**Content type:** Long-form guides (2,000-4,000 words), case-study-style explainers.

### Pillar 2: Cost & Comparison (pricing, budgets, build vs buy)

**Goal:** Capture searchers in the evaluation stage. Be the most honest, data-backed answer.

Topics include: how much does custom software cost, outsource vs in-house, fixed price vs T&M, hire developer vs agency, no-code vs custom, software RFP templates.

**Content type:** Data-driven articles with tables, calculators, ranges. Comparison posts with clear verdict.

### Pillar 3: Technology (stacks, architecture, tools)

**Goal:** Build topical authority and attract technical decision-makers (CTOs, lead devs).

Topics include: Next.js for SaaS, headless e-commerce, microservices vs monolith, multi-tenant architecture, Supabase vs Firebase, API-first design, payment integrations.

**Content type:** Technical deep-dives (1,500-3,000 words), opinionated "when to use X" posts.

### Pillar 4: AI & Automation

**Goal:** Position SystemForge as a builder of AI-powered products, not just traditional CRUD apps.

Topics include: LLM integration, RAG applications, AI agents for business, chatbot development, workflow automation, replacing spreadsheets with custom tools.

**Content type:** Use-case-driven posts, "how we built X" narratives, trend analysis.

### Pillar 5: Niche & Local

**Goal:** Capture long-tail, low-competition keywords. Drive local/niche-specific leads.

Topics include: software for healthcare (HIPAA), fintech development, real estate tech, restaurant systems, city-specific queries (New York, SF, Austin, Miami).

**Content type:** Niche landing pages, programmatic city pages, industry-specific guides.

## 4. Editorial Tone & Writing Guidelines

### Voice

- **Dev-centric:** Write like a senior engineer explaining to a peer, not like a marketing team writing ad copy.
- **Direct:** Get to the point in the first 100 words. Answer the query, then expand.
- **Action-oriented:** Every article ends with a clear next step. No "it depends" without a recommendation.
- **Honest about trade-offs:** If something is expensive, say so. If a tool has limitations, name them.

### Rules

- American English, naturally written. No British spellings.
- Second person ("you") throughout. Never "one should" or "the reader."
- Short paragraphs (2-4 sentences max). Use subheadings every 200-300 words.
- No filler phrases: avoid "in this article we will explore," "it is important to note," "without further ado."
- No rhetorical questions as openers. Start with a direct statement or a real data point.
- Technical terms without dumbing down, but define acronyms on first use.
- Include specific numbers, ranges, and timelines whenever possible (e.g., "$25K-$80K for an MVP" not "it can be expensive").
- CTAs are woven naturally into the content, not bolted on. Preferred: "If you want a second opinion on your tech stack, book a free call" over "Contact us today!"

### Formatting

- H2 for main sections, H3 for subsections. Never skip heading levels.
- Tables for comparisons. Bullet lists only when listing 4+ parallel items.
- Code blocks for technical examples (syntax-highlighted).
- FAQ section (3-5 questions) at the bottom of every article, marked with FAQ schema.
- Internal links: minimum 3 per article, contextually placed.

## 6. Competitive Landscape

### Direct competitors (dev agency blogs)

| Competitor | Strengths | Weaknesses | Our angle |
|-----------|-----------|------------|-----------|
| **Toptal Blog** | Massive DA (80+), deep technical content, strong freelancer brand | Generic "hire talent" positioning, no project-based pricing transparency | We publish real cost ranges and timelines. We build the product, not just staff it. |
| **Turing Blog** | AI-matching angle, strong SEO on "hire X developer" queries | Content is hiring-focused, not building-focused | We target "build X" not "hire X" — the reader wants a product, not a contractor. |
| **Clutch/GoodFirms listings** | High DA directory links, review-driven trust | Pay-to-play rankings, no educational content | We earn trust through depth, not badges. Link-building via genuinely useful guides. |
| **Vercel/Next.js Blog** | Massive technical authority on the framework we use | Product-focused, not service-focused | We bridge the gap: "here's the tech, and here's how to get it built for your business." |
| **Generic agency blogs (Netguru, Brainhub, Relevant Software)** | Established DA (40-60), large content libraries | Cookie-cutter "top 10 frameworks" posts, no personality, repetitive | We write with a builder's perspective, include real trade-offs, and don't hide behind platitudes. |

### Content gaps we exploit

1. **Real pricing data.** Most agency blogs say "it depends" and gate the answer behind a form. We publish ranges.
2. **AI + custom software intersection.** Few agencies blog about LLM integration, RAG, or AI agents with practical building advice.
3. **Nearshore from a builder's POV.** Content about nearshore is either from staffing firms or offshore mills. We write as the actual team doing the work.
4. **Startup-specific advice.** Most content targets enterprise. We write for the $1M-ARR founder deciding between no-code and custom.
5. **Process transparency.** We publish our actual development process (SystemForge methodology) as content, building trust through openness.

### Link-building strategy

- Guest posts on startup/SaaS communities (Indie Hackers, Hacker News, Dev.to)
- Contribute to open-source and reference in articles
- Publish data-driven posts that attract natural backlinks (cost surveys, stack comparisons)
- Repurpose top articles as LinkedIn posts (cross-promote with /mkt:linkedin-mkt)
- Build HARO/Connectively responses for software development cost queries

## 7. GEO (Generative Engine Optimization)

Per config `geo_optimization`:

- **Answer in first 100 words:** Every article opens with a direct answer to the search query. No preamble.
- **Clear H2 structure:** Headings mirror how an AI would parse the page for featured snippets.
- **Current timestamps:** Articles include "Updated for 2026" and current pricing data.
- **FAQ schema always:** Structured data on every article for rich results and AI citation.
- **Cited sources:** Link to official docs, industry reports, and verifiable data when stating facts.

## 8. Measurement & Iteration

### Monthly review cycle

1. Run `/blog:analytics-review` to generate `analytics-feed.json`
2. Classify each article: MANTER (keep), EXPANDIR (expand), REFRESH, MERGE, or KILL
3. Run `/blog:refresh-content` on flagged articles
4. Publish new Wave based on keyword gaps

### Quarterly strategy review

- Reassess ICP based on actual lead data
- Update keyword research with new volume data
- Adjust pillar weights based on conversion performance
- Evaluate competitive landscape shifts
