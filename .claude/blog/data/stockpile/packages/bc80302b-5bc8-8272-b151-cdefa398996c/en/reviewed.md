# How Much Does a Simple ERP Cost for a Small Business in 2026

**A simple ERP for a small business costs between US$8,000 and US$40,000 for custom development, or between US$200 and US$1,800 per month for SaaS platforms.** The right choice depends on your process complexity, customization requirements, and total cost of ownership over 5 years. For most SMBs with 10–80 employees, custom-built ERP pays for itself in 18–36 months compared to ongoing SaaS subscriptions.

I'm Pedro Corgnati, founder of SystemForge, with over 8 years building management systems for small and medium businesses. I've implemented ERPs for distributors, CRMs for sales teams, and integrated platforms for franchise networks. This article gives you real market numbers — not inflated agency estimates.

## What a Simple ERP Must Include

A functional ERP for a small business needs, at minimum:

- **Inventory management** with inflows, outflows, and stock counts
- **Financial module** — accounts payable, receivable, and cash flow
- **Sales and order management** with customer history
- **Tax-compliant invoicing** (US: sales tax automation; global: local tax rules)
- **Management reports** — revenue, margins, overdue accounts

The shift from "simple ERP" to "custom ERP" happens when your business has unique processes: multi-level purchase approvals, batch/lot tracking with expiration, complex commission structures, or integrations with proprietary systems.

## The Real Total Cost of Ownership: SaaS vs. Custom ERP Over 5 Years

One of the most common mistakes is comparing only the upfront costs. Here is an honest TCO comparison for a mid-size SMB (25–50 employees):

**SaaS ERP (mid-tier platform, 20 users):**
- Monthly subscription: US$900/month
- Annual price increases (average 12%): cumulative
- Year 1: US$10,800 | Year 2: US$12,096 | Year 3: US$13,548 | Year 4: US$15,173 | Year 5: US$16,994
- 5-year total: ~US$68,600
- Plus: customization add-ons (US$5,000–15,000 for features the standard plan doesn't cover)
- Data portability: limited — migrating away is expensive and risky

**Custom ERP (same functionality, owned system):**
- Development investment: US$22,000
- Monthly support/hosting: US$400/month
- 5-year total: US$22,000 + (US$400 × 60) = US$46,000
- You own the code and data. Migration to a new host costs nothing. Future enhancements are built on existing code.

The break-even point for this example is around month 30. After that, every month the custom ERP costs less in total TCO.

## When Custom ERP Development Makes Sense

Custom development is the right investment when:

**Your workflows don't fit standard platforms.** Royalty calculation for franchisees, perishable lot management with multi-supplier traceability, multi-level procurement approval, or integration with industry-specific legacy systems — none of these are well-handled by generic SaaS without expensive add-ons or workarounds.

**You're paying more than US$900/month in SaaS licenses.** That's US$54,000 over 5 years, before annual price increases of 10–15%. A custom ERP at US$16,000 with US$300/month maintenance totals US$34,000 over 5 years. You own the system and your data.

**Critical integrations aren't available off the shelf.** Connecting a SaaS ERP to your proprietary warehouse management system, logistics provider APIs, or custom e-commerce platform often requires custom development anyway — at extra cost on top of your subscription.

## What Drives ERP Development Costs Up or Down

**Cost drivers:**
- Tax-compliant invoicing automation with real-time validation (+US$3,000–8,000)
- Multi-marketplace integration (Amazon, eBay, Shopify, etc.) (+US$5,000–15,000)
- Native mobile app for sales reps (+US$14,000–22,000)
- Multi-company with consolidated financials (+US$7,000–15,000)
- Embedded BI with custom dashboards (+US$5,000–12,000)

**Cost reducers:**
- Well-documented scope before development starts (saves 20–25%)
- Simple, exception-free workflows
- No legacy system integrations required
- Internal team available for testing and validation

A company that approaches us with documented processes and clear requirements saves an average of 25% compared to projects that start without mapped workflows.

## ERP by Industry: What Each Sector Typically Pays

ERP costs vary substantially by industry because each sector has distinct technical requirements:

**Wholesale distribution:** requires lot/batch tracking with expiration dates, high-volume invoicing, freight management, and multi-warehouse inventory. Custom ERP: US$20,000–50,000. Integration with carrier APIs and tax compliance for interstate sales adds complexity.

**Retail (physical stores):** point-of-sale integration, SKU-level inventory, promotions, and loyalty programs. ERP + POS: US$12,000–30,000 for single location. Multi-location retailers add US$4,000–10,000 per additional store.

**Professional services:** project-based billing, time tracking, resource scheduling, contract management. Services ERP: US$10,000–28,000. Firms billing on retainer vs. project need different financial module logic.

**Manufacturing (light):** bill of materials, work orders, job costing, quality control. Manufacturing ERP: US$22,000–55,000. Complexity scales with BOM depth and number of production stages.

**E-commerce:** multi-channel marketplace integration (Amazon, eBay, Shopify), returns management, real-time inventory sync. E-commerce ERP: US$15,000–40,000 depending on number of channels.

**Healthcare clinics:** appointment scheduling, patient records, insurance billing, HIPAA compliance. Clinical systems: US$20,000–65,000 because of regulatory requirements and secure data handling.

Communicating your industry upfront when requesting quotes leads to far more accurate proposals — and avoids comparing apples to oranges across vendors with different sector experience.

## How to Compare ERP Quotes Without Being Misled

Receiving wildly different quotes (US$12,000 vs US$35,000 vs US$80,000 for the same project) is common. To compare them properly:

**Request module-level breakdowns.** A lump-sum quote without itemization means the vendor didn't understand your scope — or plans to charge extras later.

**Ask about post-delivery support.** A 6-month bug warranty and clear monthly maintenance contract are non-negotiable in a professional engagement.

**Demand specific milestones.** "Approximately 4 months" is not a contract. "Financial module in week 6, inventory in week 12, go-live in week 16" is.

**Ask about post-go-live support SLAs.** A professional vendor defines what "support" means contractually: response time targets (e.g., critical bugs fixed within 24 hours, non-critical within 5 business days), what qualifies as a bug vs. a new feature request, and how emergency situations are handled. Vague "we'll take care of it" promises are worthless when your ERP is down on a Monday morning.

**Check references from similar industries.** A developer who has built ERP for food distributors understands lot management and expiration logic. A developer who only built e-commerce sites will spend your budget learning your business.

**Verify source code ownership in the contract.** After full payment, the complete source code must be delivered to you. Vendors who retain the code as leverage for future maintenance contracts put your business at risk. Insist on a clause explicitly stating that all code, database schemas, and deployment credentials become your property upon final payment.

**Ask specifically about performance testing.** An ERP handling 5,000 daily transactions has different infrastructure requirements than one processing 50. Developers without experience at your scale will miscalculate database indexing, caching, and server sizing — leading to a system that slows down exactly when you need it most.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the minimum realistic cost for a reliable custom ERP?

For a basic ERP covering inventory, financials, and tax-compliant invoicing built by a professional firm, the minimum realistic investment is US$9,000–12,000. Below that, you're typically hiring a junior freelancer without documentation, support guarantees, or project continuity.

### SaaS ERP vs. custom ERP: what's the 5-year total cost?

A SaaS ERP at US$700/month totals US$42,000 over 5 years, excluding annual price increases of 10–15%. A custom ERP at US$16,000 with US$300/month maintenance totals US$34,000 over 5 years — and you own the system and data permanently.

### How long does it take to build an ERP for a small business?

A basic ERP (financials + inventory + sales) takes 3–5 months. With tax invoicing and custom reports: 4–7 months. Full ERP with mobile app and BI: 6–12 months. A well-defined scope at the start reduces timeline by up to 30%.

### Is there a free ERP that actually works for small businesses?

Yes. ERPNext (open source) is functional and widely deployed globally. The license is free — you pay for hosting, implementation (US$4,000–18,000), and support. For businesses with moderately complex processes, ERPNext implementation often costs as much as a mid-range custom build, without the exact fit.

### Do I need both ERP and CRM?

Product and distribution companies prioritize ERP. Service companies with active sales teams prioritize CRM. Companies with long sales cycles and complex operations need both, integrated. A unified system handling both is typically the most cost-effective long-term choice.

### What is the monthly cost of running a custom ERP after delivery?

Beyond the support retainer (US$200–500/month), ongoing operational costs include: cloud server hosting (US$80–300/month depending on size), SSL certificates and security patching, and periodic updates when tax APIs change (typically 1–2 times per year). All-in monthly cost for a mid-size SMB: US$300–800/month. Factor this into your 5-year total cost comparison.

### How does data migration from the old system work?

Data migration is one of the most critical and underestimated phases. It includes: analysis and mapping of data from the legacy system, format conversion, inconsistency cleanup, staged import with verification, and team training on the new system. Average migration timeline: 2–6 weeks depending on data volume. Professional developers include migration in their scope — but the client needs to assign someone who understands the legacy data to validate results.

### What happens if the development company closes down?

This is why code ownership is non-negotiable. If the development company ceases operations, you need full possession of: the source code repository, database schema and backups, server credentials, and documentation. Any vendor that withholds these is building leverage over you. Always verify this is explicitly written in the contract before signing.
