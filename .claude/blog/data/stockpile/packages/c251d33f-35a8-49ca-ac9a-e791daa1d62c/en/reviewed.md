## What Makes Technical Consulting Actually Good

Quality technical consulting goes far beyond a generic opinion. It delivers:

- **Situational diagnosis**: maps current architecture, bottlenecks, technical debt, and security risks.
- **Technology roadmap**: defines technologies, integrations, incremental scope, and prioritization criteria.
- **Realistic estimates**: presents costs, timelines, and team needs based on comparable projects.
- **Execution support**: follows sprints, reviews deliveries, and adjusts course when needed.

The real differentiator is who leads the work. Consultancies led by professionals who have shipped production systems provide more applicable and less theoretical recommendations. In the U.S. market, this matters even more because every industry has its own regulatory, infrastructure, and user-behavior nuances.

## Most Common Mistakes When Hiring Technical Consulting

**1. Hiring without implementation references**
A consultant who only recommends but has never built can deliver beautiful theory that does not apply. Ask for case studies and, when possible, speak with former clients.

**2. Confusing consulting with project sales**
If every recommendation points to one product or stack, there may be a commercial bias. Good consulting prioritizes the client's problem.

**3. Ignoring culture and processes**
Technology alone does not solve problems. Consultancies that ignore the internal team, digital maturity, and workflows produce recommendations that do not stick.

**4. Failing to document decisions**
Technical decisions without records become future technical debt. Demand clear deliverables: diagrams, architecture decision records (ADRs), and a roadmap.

## When to Hire vs. Solve Internally

**Hiring technical consulting makes sense when:**
- The internal team is overloaded or lacks experience with that type of project.
- The decision involves high investment and mistakes are expensive.
- An external opinion is needed to validate technical direction.
- The timeline is tight and there is no room for trial and error.

**Solving internally works better when:**
- The team already has mature domain expertise.
- The need is small and recurring.
- There is time for controlled experimentation.

The choice does not have to be binary. Many companies use technical consulting strategically for key decisions while keeping day-to-day execution in-house.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is technical consulting in software projects?
Technical consulting is a specialized service that analyzes a project's architecture, processes, and technologies to recommend the best execution path, reducing risks and costs.

### How much does technical consulting cost in the United States?
Investment typically ranges from $2,500 to $15,000 for focused engagements, depending on complexity. Monthly advisory retainers usually fall between $5,000 and $10,000.

### What is the difference between technical consulting and code audit?
A code audit focuses on reviewing what has already been built. Technical consulting goes further by aligning architecture, technology choices, roadmap, and execution with business goals.

### How long does technical consulting take to deliver value?
An initial diagnosis takes 3 to 5 days. A complete architecture and roadmap engagement can be delivered in 1 to 2 weeks.

### Is it worth hiring technical consulting before building a system?
Yes. Companies that invest in technical consulting before development avoid bad architectural choices, reduce rework, and accelerate time-to-market.
